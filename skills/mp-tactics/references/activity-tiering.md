# Activity tiering framework

Tiering decides how much effort any piece of marketing gets: a launch, a campaign, a content piece, a partnership, an announcement. It removes the "how big a deal is this?" debate by setting the scope **once the tier is decided**.

## Default tiers (calibrate to the brand)

| Tier | What qualifies | Frequency | Treatment |
|------|----------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Tier 1: Flagship** | A new offering, a major moment, a rebrand, the biggest-name partnership | 1–2 a year | Full multi-channel campaign: press, partners, paid, every owned channel, lead-in and follow-up, senior involvement |
| **Tier 2: Feature** | Significant moments with real impact on objectives | A few a quarter | A dedicated content set across core channels, newsletter feature, partner or guest amplification, possibly paid boost |
| **Tier 3: Standard** | Regular output that serves the audience | Weekly or monthly | Standard pipeline: core-channel posts in the normal formats, no extra sign-off |
| **Tier 4: Always-on** | Small updates, evergreen reuse, maintenance | Continuous | Single post, story, changelog or reshare only |

**Calibrate:**
- Small team or low output: merge to three tiers (Flagship / Feature / Standard) and explain why.
- High-volume brand: consider a self-serve layer where anyone can publish Tier 4 without review.
- Deliverables per tier must come from the brand's **core channels** and fit its stated capacity. Don't list channels the team can't sustain.

For each tier, define: assets and channels · who's responsible · lead time · sign-off needed · a one-line description that anyone in the business would understand.

## Assessment questions (default five; adapt)

1. **Impact on objectives:** will it move a key result in `05-objectives.md`? Which one, and by how much?
2. **Audience demand:** how much does the target audience want this? (Requests, comments, search interest, past performance of similar pieces.)
3. **Breadth:** how much of the audience is it relevant to? The whole base or a niche?
4. **Story fit:** does it reinforce the positioning and messaging pillars?
5. **Timing and context:** what else is happening? (Other campaigns, seasonality, cultural moments, competitor moves, capacity that week.)

Adapt them to context. For example, an early-stage brand might weight competitive dynamics more. A sponsor-funded brand might add "commercial value to partners".

## Scoring guide

Score each question 1–3 (low / medium / high). Then:

| Total (5 questions) | Tier |
|---------------------|------|
| 13–15 | Tier 1 |
| 10–12 | Tier 2 |
| 7–9 | Tier 3 |
| 5–6 | Tier 4 |

These are guides, not laws. Borderline calls (especially between Tier 2 and 3) should say what would tip it either way. Don't inflate a tier. If it's a Tier 3, call it a Tier 3.

## Process notes

- Assign the tier at the planning stage, not when the work is ready to go out.
- Agree it between the Marketing Manager and whoever owns the thing being marketed.
- Record why: keep the five scores next to each backlog item.
