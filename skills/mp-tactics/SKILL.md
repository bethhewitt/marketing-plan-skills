# Tactics (mp-tactics)

Read `../marketing-plan/references/conventions.md` (the shared conventions file in the `marketing-plan` skill, a sibling of this skill's folder) and `references/activity-tiering.md` (in this skill's folder) first. Then run the dependency check. This skill needs: 3, 4, 5, 6, 7.

Tactics answer "what do we actually make, where, and how much effort does it get?" They stop short of scheduling. The 90-day roadmap, metrics and backlog belong to the Marketing Manager.

## Part A: Content pillars

Content pillars are the 3–5 recurring themes the brand publishes about. They're **not** the same as messaging value pillars. Value pillars are what we claim. Content pillars are what we make so the audience experiences those claims.

1. Propose 3–5 content pillars. For each one, give:
   - **Name and one-line description**
   - **Value pillar or differentiator it proves** (from 07). Every content pillar must trace to one.
   - **Audience job it serves** (from 04): inform, inspire, entertain, help decide, belong
   - **Motion it mainly serves:** Brand / Land / Expand
   - **Formats:** e.g. short clips, carousels, long-form, newsletter, live, UGC
   - **Example pieces:** 3, specific to this brand
   - **Mix:** rough share of output (e.g. 40%)
2. Check the mix against the objective weighting in 05. If Expand carries 60% of the objectives but only 20% of the content serves Expand, flag it.
3. Confirm the pillars with the user, then the mix.

## Part B: Channels

1. List candidate channels across owned (site, newsletter, the show itself), shared (social platforms, communities), earned (press, guest appearances, partnerships, word of mouth) and paid. Use the "where they spend time" detail in 04, the platform shifts in 03 and the activation plans in 06.
2. Give each channel **a single primary role**. Be honest about where the audience actually is and what the team can sustain (capacity from the brief).

| Channel | Role (Brand / Land / Expand) | Target segment | Content pillars | Formats | Cadence (sustainable) | Success signal | Priority (core / test / not now) |

3. Recommend at most 3 **core** channels and 1–2 **test** channels for the period. Put everything else in "Not now", with a reason. Confirm with the user.
4. Note channel-specific best practice only where the project has a knowledge file for it. Link to the file rather than restating it.

## Part C: Pillar × channel matrix

A grid with content pillars as rows and core and test channels as columns. Each cell shows the format, which campaign line from 07 to lead with, and the motion. Leave a cell empty when a pillar doesn't belong on a channel. Empty cells are a decision, not a gap.

## Part D: Activity tiering

Set up the tiering framework from `references/activity-tiering.md`, calibrated to this brand:
1. Ask about the context it needs (only what the brief doesn't already answer): team size, publishing frequency, cross-functional support, and realistic capacity per channel.
2. Propose the tiers (the default is four; recommend merging to three for small teams or low output), with the deliverables per tier drawn from the core channels above.
3. Agree the assessment questions and the scoring guide.
4. Confirm each tier with the user.

The Marketing Manager then uses this to tier every backlog item. Don't add deliverables beyond what each tier specifies.

## Part E: Tactical ideas pool

List the tactical ideas that came up across sections 1–8 (activation plans, campaign lines, competitor whitespace, pressure-test actions). For each one, give the idea, its content pillar, channel, motion and suggested tier. **Don't prioritise or schedule them.** This is raw material for the Marketing Manager's backlog.
