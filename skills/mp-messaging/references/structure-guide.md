# Messaging structure guide

The worked examples use **Harbour & Hearth**, an invented independent coffee roaster with a subscription. They're illustrative only. Write each brand's own version in its own voice.

## Layer 1: core message at four lengths

### Tagline (3–8 words)
Pattern: {core value or transformation}, {qualifier or who}.
The sharpest articulation. It must be specific enough that it wouldn't fit a competitor.
- Weak: "Great coffee, delivered." (Anyone could say it.)
- Stronger: "Small-batch coffee, roasted the week you drink it."

### One sentence
Pattern: {Brand} is {category} for {who}, {what changes, in the audience's language}.
> Harbour & Hearth is a coffee subscription for people who've outgrown supermarket beans, with small-batch roasts delivered within seven days of roasting so every cup tastes the way the roaster meant it to.

### ~100 words
1. Open with the one sentence (or a close variant).
2. Add 1–2 sentences on how it works or what makes it possible.
3. Add proof of scale or credibility.
4. Close by reinforcing the core value.

### ~200 words
1. Core message.
2. Market context: why this approach is different or needed now.
3. More on how it works.
4. Expanded proof.
5. What else: formats, partners, the wider world of the brand.
6. A closing line that reinforces the positioning.

## Layer 2: value pillars

Use three, or four if there are genuinely four distinct dimensions. Two feels thin. Five or more usually overlap.

**Name:** short. Either outcome-style ("Freshness you can taste") or imperative ("Never run out"). Don't mix the two styles.

**Narrative (2–4 sentences):** the problem first, then the brand.
> Supermarket coffee can sit on a shelf for months, and by the time you open it most of what made it special has gone. Harbour & Hearth roasts to order and ships within seven days, so the bag you open is at its best.

**Proof points:** bullets that lead with the capability or evidence and end with the impact.
- Roast date printed on every bag, so you always know how fresh it is.
- {Gap: customer retention or review number needed}

Common pillar themes (always name them in the brand's own terms): ease, quality, belonging or identity, discovery, value, trust.

## Layer 3: differentiators

Pillars are the value we deliver. Differentiators are why we deliver it better than the alternatives. Each has a headline and 2–3 sentences, plus optional evidence bullets.

Common patterns: **access** (people, places or content only we have) · **expertise or taste** (who's behind it) · **track record** (scale, longevity, awards) · **format or experience** · **point of view**. Use whichever is actually true. These are prompts, not a checklist.

## Layer 4: persona messaging

Personas are defined in `04-icp.md`. Reference them by name. For each:

- **Lead message:** how you'd describe the brand to this person in one line if you met them.
- **One sentence:** reframed for what they care about.
- **{Pillar} for {persona}:** the same pillar names, but a different angle and problem.
- **Proof that matters to them:** the same evidence base, surfacing different metrics.

The test: if you swap the persona name and the copy still works, you haven't rewritten it.

## Layer 5: campaign lines

5–10 short lines for ads, social, email and subject lines. Each one is tagged:

| Line | Pillar | Motion (Brand / Land / Expand) | Suggested use |

Optionally add content ideas (format + angle + pillar) or use cases (headline + scenario + how the brand helps) when the brand has distinct contexts of use.
