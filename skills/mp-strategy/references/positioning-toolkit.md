# Positioning toolkit

## The four tests

Good positioning is:
- **Clear:** easy to understand and remember.
- **Differentiated:** distinct in ways that matter to the audience.
- **Credible:** true and provable today.
- **Strategic:** it guides decisions about the offering, content, partnerships and spend.

## Longlist angles

Sort every option under the statement slots, sub-grouped by angle:

- **Who is it for?** By demographic or role · by characteristic (life stage, company type) · by mindset or identity · by moment or stage.
- **What is it?** Format language ("a podcast", "a platform") · function language ("a travel planning tool") · outcome language ("your next trip, decided") · transformation or identity language ("for people who travel to be changed").
- **What value?** Functional · emotional · social · economic.
- **Why us?** Access (who and what only we have) · expertise or taste · format or experience · track record or proof · values or point of view.
- **Unlike...?** Unlike incumbents · unlike challengers · unlike point solutions or substitutes · unlike doing nothing.

Keep a **parking lot** at the bottom for everything you reject. Rejected ideas often resurface as messaging later.

## Where previous positioning fails (check before routes)

- Is there a gap between the strategy documents and the customer-facing copy? That gap is usually the story problem.
- Do the people who represent the brand tell a different version from the official one? Where they diverge is where the official story fails them.
- Did the last attempt fail for a reason that's still true? If so, name it and design around it.

## Prompts to reuse

**Objection test.** "Here is a positioning route: {route}. Argue against it. Which competitor could claim this with equal or greater credibility? Which part is not currently provable? Where does it describe the category rather than take a point of view? What does choosing this route cost us? Be blunt."

**Testing against real language.** When transcripts, comments, reviews or survey answers exist, check each value idea against them. How often does it appear, and in whose words? What language does the audience actually use? Is it supported, weakly supported, or absent? What comes up repeatedly that the positioning doesn't cover?

## Team-mode alignment plan

Fill this in before the work starts. The order of conversations matters as much as their content.

| Stage | Who | Format | Purpose | Output |
|-------|-----|--------|---------|--------|
| Rules of play | Sponsor / decision-maker | 1:1 | Who decides, deadlines | Named decision-maker, rollout date |
| Discovery | 5–15 stakeholders | 1:1 interviews | Gather views on the same questions | Notes, agreements and disagreements |
| Diagnosis playback | Leadership | Group | Agree the problem, not the answer | Agreed problem statement |
| Fundamentals | Leadership | Group | Agree audience, category, competition | Questions 1–3 in writing |
| Routes | Each decision-maker | 1:1 | Eliminate weak routes, test them blind | Chosen route |
| Pressure test | Audience + frontline staff | 1:1 | True? Clear? Changes thinking? | Final tweaks |
| Socialise | All teams that will use it | Preview sessions | Understanding before launch | Bought-in team |
