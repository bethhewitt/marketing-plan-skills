# Playbook structure

The compiled `playbook.md` is a **tight summary of the plan, not an in-depth analysis**. It shows where the brand is going and how marketing gets it there, section by section, in a form someone can read in one sitting and act on. The section files and `working/` hold the depth: research, evidence, profiles, reasoning and detail. The playbook distils them.

## Length and style

- **Aim for 3,000 to 4,500 words in total.** About 1 to 2 A4 pages per section. If a section runs longer, it's carrying analysis that belongs in its section file.
- **Summarise, don't lift.** Rewrite each approved section down to its decisions and the direction they set. Keep approved wording for the lines that matter exactly (mission, vision, positioning statement, tagline, objectives, key results), but condense everything around them.
- **One idea, one place.** Say each thing once. Don't repeat the positioning, objectives or audience in later sections; refer back instead.
- **Prefer a compact table or a short list to paragraphs.** One table per topic, not one per item. Never leave blank cells to group rows; use a subheading per group instead, so tables stay aligned when they split across pages.
- **Plain, warm and direct**, in the brand's voice and following the project's writing rules.
- **No research appendix.** Bring forward only the facts that explain the plan: the brand's own position in section 1 under "Where things stand", and market shifts in section 3, "The landscape".

## Template

```markdown
# {Brand} Marketing Playbook: {plan name}

Owner: {plan owner} · Period: {timeframe} · Compiled: {date}

## How to use this playbook
Two short paragraphs: what this is and how the sections build on each other; who signs it off, what follows (roadmap, backlog), and that the research and reasoning live in the section files.

## Plan on a page
Must fit on one A4 page. Leave out anything that pushes it over.
- Mission · Vision · Who we're for · Positioning · Tagline (one line each)
- Objectives (headlines only)
- Content pillars (names and mix) · Priority channels and tests

## 1. Brief
- The offering: 2–3 bullets
- What the business needs, in priority order: 3–5 one-line bullets
- Where things stand: 3–5 facts about the brand's own performance (market facts go in section 3)
- How we'll work: budget, team, constraints, as 3–5 bullets
- Key moments of the period: one line

## 2. Mission & vision
Mission, vision, beliefs (one line each) and 1–2 sentences on what this means for the period.

## 3. The landscape
One line on the state of the category, then one table of 4–6 market shifts (what's happening · what it means for us). Each row carries one sourced fact and ties to a decision elsewhere in the plan (a channel, objective or tactic); drop any trend that doesn't change a decision. One "Sources:" line underneath.

## 4. Competitors
One table: competitor · what they offer · what it means for us (one row each). Then "Where we can win" as 3–5 bullets.

## 5. Who we're for
Per segment: who they are, what they want, where we find them (one line each), personas as one line each. One "Not for" line.

## 6. Objectives
One line on where the weight goes. Each objective as a subheading with its own short table (key result · now · target), not one big table with blank cells under each objective. One "Not this period" line.

## 7. Strategy
The positioning statement. "How we win": one short paragraph per motion (Brand, Land, Expand). The flywheel in 2–3 sentences. One line on the pressure test.

## 8. Messaging
Tagline, one sentence, the ~100-word version. Value pillars as one table (value · what it means · proof). "Why us" in one paragraph. Campaign lines as one table (line · use), 5–8 lines.

## 9. Tactics
Content pillars as one table (pillar · what it is · example · mix). Channels as one table (channel · its job · sustainable cadence), plus one line each for tests and "not now". Recommendations as a numbered list, 1–3 lines each. Activity tiers as one table (tier · for · what it gets · lead time) and one line on how to score.

## 10. Metrics
One scorecard table (key result · now · checkpoints · target). Tests as one table (test · keep going if · decide by). Rhythm as 3 bullets.

## Next steps
5–8 numbered actions that get the plan moving, starting with sign-off and baselines.

## What we still need
The 5–8 open questions that most affect the plan, as a table (question · why it matters). The full gap list stays in the section files.
```

## What stays out of the playbook

The playbook reads as decided, not as a record of deciding, and as a summary, not an analysis. These stay in the section files and `working/`:

| Leave out | Typical source |
|---|---|
| The research pack, evidence tables, data analysis and desk-research sources | 01, 03 |
| Full competitor profiles, differentiation maps and whitespace reasoning (keep one competitor table and "where we can win") | 03 |
| Market trends that don't change a decision in the plan, and the evidence column of the landscape table | 01, 03 |
| Detailed segment profiles: jobs to be done, triggers, barriers, language, evidence keys (keep who / want / where) | 04 |
| Persona messaging, the ~200-word version and section-level gap tables | 07 |
| The pillar × channel matrix, the tactical ideas pool and tier assessment detail | 08 |
| Measurement rules, diagnostic metrics and the monthly log template | 09 |
| "Options considered" and every rejected or alternative option | 02, 07 |
| Scoring and selection tables that justify a choice | 04 |
| Positioning longlists, routes, objection tests and statement test tables | 06, `working/` |
| "Positioning implications", emerging hypotheses and anything written for a later section | 01, 03 |
| Mix checks, consistency-check results and rationale for process choices | 08, framework |
| Mode lines, version notes, "Decision (date):" labels and "decided in session" wording | all |
| Inputs lists, provenance columns, file names and `working/` paths (say "section N" instead) | all |

**Check before saving:**
- Word count is between 3,000 and 4,500.
- Nothing is said twice.
- A search for "considered", "route", "objection", "scoring", "chosen", "rejected", "longlist", "Mode:", "v1.", "appendix" and `working/` finds nothing that isn't part of the plan itself.
- Every figure matches the approved section files.
